Overview

Vanderbilt's transfer acceptance rate has fluctuated meaningfully year to year, generally landing somewhere between the high-teens and low-20s in recent cycles — with about 450–700 transfer students enrolling each fall. That's notably more accessible than Vanderbilt's Regular Decision first-year rate, which has fallen into the low single digits for recent classes. For students with a strong first year at another institution, transfer can genuinely be the stronger path into a school like Vanderbilt.

Vanderbilt accepts transfer applicants for fall entry only, reviews on a rolling basis after the priority deadline through May 1, and is need-blind for U.S. citizens and eligible non-citizens, meeting 100% of demonstrated need for admitted transfers.

What Vanderbilt Looks For

  • Strong, consistent academic performance in your first year of college coursework — generally a B+/A- average or higher among admitted students
  • A specific, well-argued reason why Vanderbilt fits your academic and personal trajectory better than your current school
  • Full-time enrollment and engagement at your current institution, since Vanderbilt is a residential campus community
  • Fit with the specific school you're applying to — Arts & Science, Engineering, Peabody, or Blair each evaluate differently, and Blair requires an audition

Requirements & Deadlines

  • Common Application or Coalition Application for Transfer, including personal essay and short answers
  • Priority deadline of February 15; applications considered on a rolling basis until May 1
  • Two academic letters of recommendation, ideally including one from a college professor
  • College Report and official transcripts from every institution attended
  • SAT/ACT scores optional for fall 2026 and 2027 entry
