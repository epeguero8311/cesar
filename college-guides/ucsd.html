Decision dates, requirements, and what the admissions committee actually prioritizes — for both first-year and transfer applicants.

Overview

University of California, San Diego's first-year admissions process is evaluated separately from its transfer process, with its own applicant pool, review committee, and timeline. Most highly selective schools admit first-year applicants at a meaningfully different rate than transfers, so the numbers above should not be assumed to mirror the transfer figures below.

Applicants generally apply through Early Decision, Early Action, or Regular Decision depending on the school's specific offerings, and should weigh the tradeoffs of each round carefully, since binding Early Decision plans and non-binding Early Action or Restrictive Early Action plans have different implications for financial aid and other applications.

What UC San Diego Looks For

  • A rigorous high school course load, including the most advanced classes available (AP, IB, honors, or dual enrollment)
  • Strong, consistent grades throughout high school, particularly in junior and senior year
  • Meaningful extracurricular involvement, leadership, or personal responsibilities that show depth rather than a long list of shallow activities
  • Thoughtful, authentic essays that reveal genuine voice and self-reflection
  • Strong letters of recommendation from teachers and counselors who know the applicant well

Requirements & Deadlines

  • Common Application, Coalition Application, or the school's own first-year application, submitted by the relevant deadline above
  • Official high school transcript and school profile
  • Letters of recommendation from a counselor and one or more teachers
  • Standardized testing policy varies by school and by year — confirm current SAT/ACT requirements before applying
  • Decisions released according to the applicant's chosen round (Early Decision/Action vs. Regular Decision)

Overview

UC San Diego runs one of the highest transfer acceptance rates among major research universities, generally in the 50–55% range in recent cycles — for fall 2025, roughly 23,000 applicants saw about a 52.7% admit rate, with over 12,000 students admitted. Around a third of UCSD's entire undergraduate population arrived as transfers.

UCSD enrolls transfer students at the junior level only, and admits for fall entry exclusively. The large majority of admits — over 90% in recent years — come from California community colleges, though rates vary considerably by major: highly selective programs like Business Economics, Biological Sciences, Data Science, Engineering, and Public Health are considerably more competitive than the overall average.

What UC San Diego Looks For

  • Minimum 60 UC-transferable semester credits (90 quarter units) completed, since UCSD only admits at the junior level
  • Completion of major preparation courses specific to the applicant's intended program, which vary significantly by major
  • A GPA in the 3.45–3.92 mid-50% range for recently enrolled transfers, well above the 2.4 minimum (2.8 for non-residents)
  • Completion of UC transfer general education requirements, including the 7-course pattern
  • A strong response to the required personal insight questions describing preparation for the intended major

Requirements & Deadlines

  • University of California Application, opening August 1 and due by November 30 for fall entry
  • Minimum 60 UC-transferable semester units (90 quarter units) completed by the end of spring term prior to enrollment
  • Personal insight question responses (UCSD does not use letters of recommendation or official transcripts in initial review)
  • Supplemental materials for select majors, such as a portfolio or audition for Visual Arts, Music, or Theatre & Dance
  • Decisions posted to the applicant portal by April 30
