Overview

University of Florida's first-year admissions process is evaluated separately from its transfer process, with its own applicant pool, review committee, and timeline. Most highly selective schools admit first-year applicants at a meaningfully different rate than transfers, so the numbers above should not be assumed to mirror the transfer figures below.

Applicants generally apply through Early Decision, Early Action, or Regular Decision depending on the school's specific offerings, and should weigh the tradeoffs of each round carefully, since binding Early Decision plans and non-binding Early Action or Restrictive Early Action plans have different implications for financial aid and other applications.

What Florida Looks For

  • A rigorous high school course load, including the most advanced classes available (AP, IB, honors, or dual enrollment)
  • Strong, consistent grades throughout high school, particularly in junior and senior year
  • Meaningful extracurricular involvement, leadership, or personal responsibilities that show depth rather than a long list of shallow activities
  • Thoughtful, authentic essays that reveal genuine voice and self-reflection
  • Strong letters of recommendation from teachers and counselors who know the applicant well

Requirements & Deadlines

  • Common Application, Coalition Application, or the school's own first-year application, submitted by the relevant deadline above
  • Official high school transcript and school profile
  • Letters of recommendation from a counselor and one or more teachers
  • Standardized testing policy varies by school and by year — confirm current SAT/ACT requirements before applying
  • Decisions released according to the applicant's chosen round (Early Decision/Action vs. Regular Decision)

Overview

UF's transfer acceptance rate has held remarkably steady around 47% in recent cycles, a stark contrast to a freshman acceptance rate that has fallen below 25%. The university admits 2,000-3,000 qualified transfer applicants most years.

UF prioritizes applicants from the Florida College System, with roughly two-thirds of admitted transfers coming directly from a community college and about 72% having already completed an associate's degree. The university offers fall, spring, and summer transfer terms.

What Florida Looks For

  • At least 60 credit hours completed (UF strongly prioritizes incoming juniors) with a minimum 2.0 overall GPA and 2.0 in the last institution attended
  • Completion of the required prerequisite coursework for the applicant's intended major and college
  • Demonstrated foreign language competency, typically via two years of high school coursework or equivalent
  • A single major declared on the application, since changing majors after transfer admission is extremely limited
  • Particularly strong preparation for competitive programs like Business Administration, Nursing, and Engineering

Requirements & Deadlines

  • UF's online transfer application and $30 fee, submitted six to nine months before the intended term
  • Transcripts from every institution attended, regardless of credit earned
  • SAT/ACT with Writing scores required for freshman/sophomore-level transfers and certain certification programs
  • Foreign language proficiency documentation
  • Decisions released around June 15 for fall entry, November 15 for spring, and April 1 for summer, all on a rolling basis
