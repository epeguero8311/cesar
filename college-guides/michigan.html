Overview

University of Michigan's first-year admissions process is evaluated separately from its transfer process, with its own applicant pool, review committee, and timeline. Most highly selective schools admit first-year applicants at a meaningfully different rate than transfers, so the numbers above should not be assumed to mirror the transfer figures below.

Applicants generally apply through Early Decision, Early Action, or Regular Decision depending on the school's specific offerings, and should weigh the tradeoffs of each round carefully, since binding Early Decision plans and non-binding Early Action or Restrictive Early Action plans have different implications for financial aid and other applications.

What Michigan Looks For

  • A rigorous high school course load, including the most advanced classes available (AP, IB, honors, or dual enrollment)
  • Strong, consistent grades throughout high school, particularly in junior and senior year
  • Meaningful extracurricular involvement, leadership, or personal responsibilities that show depth rather than a long list of shallow activities
  • Thoughtful, authentic essays that reveal genuine voice and self-reflection
  • Strong letters of recommendation from teachers and counselors who know the applicant well

Requirements & Deadlines

  • Common Application, Coalition Application, or the school's own first-year application, submitted by the relevant deadline above
  • Official high school transcript and school profile
  • Letters of recommendation from a counselor and one or more teachers
  • Standardized testing policy varies by school and by year — confirm current SAT/ACT requirements before applying
  • Decisions released according to the applicant's chosen round (Early Decision/Action vs. Regular Decision)

Overview

The University of Michigan welcomes more than 2,000 transfer applicants to its Ann Arbor campus most years, with acceptance rates generally in the 30s — for fall 2024, roughly 6,800 students applied and about 2,360 were admitted.

Michigan is unusual in offering transfer admission across all four terms — fall, winter, spring, and summer — through its Office of Undergraduate Admissions, though this only covers the College of Literature, Science, and the Arts (LSA) and the College of Engineering. Other schools and colleges, like the Ross School of Business, set their own separate deadlines and processes.

What Michigan Looks For

  • A strong cumulative GPA, class rank where available, and the overall quality and rigor of the applicant's coursework
  • For Engineering applicants, completion (or clear in-progress/planned status) of required prerequisite coursework with a 3.0 GPA or higher
  • A well-articulated statement addressing the applicant's reasons for transferring and their academic objectives
  • Two years or more of transferable graded college coursework for the most competitive candidates
  • Coursework aligned with LSA's general education expectations for applicants without a declared engineering path

Requirements & Deadlines

  • Common Application, due February 1 for spring, summer, and fall entry, or October 1 for winter entry
  • Official transcripts from every college attended, plus a final high school transcript or GED results
  • Two required essays, including the University of Michigan-specific supplement
  • SAT/ACT scores optional (only high-school-era test scores are accepted)
  • Decisions released on a rolling basis — spring/summer/fall decisions between mid-March and early April, winter decisions as early as November
