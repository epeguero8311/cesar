Harvard College Admissions Guide

Decision dates, requirements, and what the admissions committee actually prioritizes — for both first-year and transfer applicants.

Why Consider Harvard

Being a student here means having the resources to explore nearly any academic or career pathway at the highest level. Even in fields where Harvard is not traditionally viewed as the strongest institution, such as certain areas of computer science or engineering, the university's global reputation, research ecosystem, and alumni network help its graduates remain highly competitive worldwide.

The Natural Sciences benefit from a legacy of more than 160 Nobel Prize-winning affiliates, one of the world's largest university endowments, and a location just minutes from Boston. That proximity provides students with access to one of the world's largest concentrations of pharmaceutical, biotechnology, genomics, and healthcare organizations. Undergraduates can also pursue research opportunities throughout Harvard's research and medical ecosystem, including on its Cambridge campus, at the Broad Institute (a joint research institute with MIT), Harvard Medical School, the Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health, and affiliated teaching hospitals such as Massachusetts General Hospital.

For students interested in the Humanities and Social Sciences, Harvard offers one of the world's richest academic ecosystems. With numerous Pulitzer Prize-winning faculty and alumni, students have opportunities to learn directly from distinguished scholars during office hours. Harvard has also produced numerous U.S. Supreme Court justices and influential legal leaders. At the same time, policymakers, diplomats, and global leaders regularly visit campus through institutions such as the Harvard Kennedy School and the Weatherhead Center for International Affairs.

Harvard's influence also extends just as strongly into finance and entrepreneurship. The university is widely regarded as one of Wall Street's premier target schools, with leading investment banks, consulting firms, and investment managers recruiting heavily on campus. In entrepreneurship, Harvard's proximity to both Boston's startup ecosystem and its Silicon Valley alumni network creates abundant opportunities for students to connect with investors, join accelerators such as Y Combinator, and launch early-stage ventures.

A few other interesting facts about Harvard: it consistently ranks among the world's leading universities for producing billionaire alumni, offers seamless cross-registration with MIT, and features the iconic residential House system. Under this system, first-year students live in Harvard Yard before transitioning to one of 12 residential Houses for their remaining three years, helping shape campus culture and fostering a strong sense of community.

Beyond academic and career opportunities, Harvard also boasts one of the most historic athletic traditions in the country. Anchored by the legendary Harvard–Yale rivalry, the university sponsors 42 NCAA Division I varsity teams (the most of any NCAA Division I institution). This ensures athletics and school spirit remain as integral parts of campus life alongside academics.

One Harvard tradition every visitor should know about is the John Harvard Statue in Harvard Yard. Despite its nickname, the Statue of Three Lies, the figure is not actually John Harvard, he was not the university's founder, and the inscription lists the wrong founding year (1638 instead of Harvard's actual founding year of 1636). It's one of the most well-known stories shared on campus tours and a fun piece of Harvard trivia for first-time visitors!

Overview

Harvard College remains one of the most competitive first-year admissions processes in the world. For the Class of 2029, Harvard admitted just over 2,000 students from tens of thousands of applicants, resulting in a projected overall acceptance rate of approximately 3.5%.

Applicants this past cycle could choose between Restrictive Early Action (REA), a non-binding option due November 1 with decisions released in mid-December, or Regular Decision (RD), due January 1 with decisions traditionally released on Ivy Day (the last Thursday of March). Both options allow admitted students until May 1 to make their enrollment decision.

Harvard evaluates applicants holistically: academic strength, extracurricular impact, personal character, and the context an applicant is coming from all factor into the decision.

What Harvard Looks For

  • Top-of-class academic performance in the most rigorous courses available to the applicant (successful applicants typically have an unweighted GPA between 3.9–4.0)
  • SAT or ACT scores (Harvard has reinstated standardized testing requirements), with the most competitive applicants typically scoring in the 1510–1580 SAT range or 34–36 ACT range
  • Meaningful depth in extracurriculars, leadership, or personal responsibilities rather than a long list of shallow activities
  • Essays that reveal genuine voice, personal growth, and how the applicant would contribute to the Harvard community. Successful applicants often demonstrate intellectual curiosity, resilience, a desire to make a meaningful impact, and a commitment to supporting their communities
  • Strong letters of recommendation from two teachers in different subjects

Requirements & Deadlines

  • Common Application plus Harvard's supplement
  • REA due November 1 (decisions released in mid-December) or RD due January 1 (decisions released in late March)
  • SAT or ACT scores required, with limited exceptions for demonstrated access issues
  • Two teacher recommendations and a school report/counselor recommendation
  • Enrollment reply deadline of May 1 for admitted students

Overview

Harvard College continues to be one of the most selective institutions in the nation for transfer applicants. Historically, the college has admitted only 10 to 20 transfer students from applicant pools of approximately 1,300 to 1,600, resulting in an acceptance rate historically around 1% or lower. In the most recent admissions cycle, Harvard received more than 2,000 transfer applications and admitted just 12 students!

Given the extremely limited number of available spaces, successful transfer applicants typically present outstanding academic records, a compelling and well-defined reason for seeking to transfer, and a demonstrated history of meaningful impact both inside and outside the classroom.

Harvard accepts transfer applications for fall enrollment only. Applicants must submit all materials by March 1, and admissions decisions are typically released in early May.

What Harvard Looks For

  • Outstanding academic performance in a rigorous college curriculum, typically with grades near the top of the applicant's class while also having strong highschool background (Average GPA for successful applicants is typically between 3.9 and 4.0)
  • Hyper-specific reasoning of why transferring to Harvard is essential to the applicant's academic and intellectual goals
  • Strong engagement outside the classroom through leadership, research, service, entrepreneurship, or other meaningful contributions
  • Positive academic and disciplinary standing at the current institution, supported by the required College Report
  • Thoughtful application essays that clearly explain the applicant's educational journey, reasons for transferring, and fit with Harvard's academic community

Requirements & Deadlines

  • Common Application for Transfer, including Harvard's transfer supplement, due March 1
  • Official transcripts from all colleges attended, along with a final high school transcript
  • College Report completed by the applicant's current institution and an Academic Evaluation from college instructors
  • Midterm Report (although not officially required, it is highly recommended to demonstrate continued strong academic performance)
  • SAT or ACT scores (Harvard reinstated testing requirements), typically in the 1510-1580 range / 34-36 range for the most competitive applicants
  • Admission decisions historically released in early May for fall enrollment
