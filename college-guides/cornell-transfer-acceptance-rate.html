Overview

Cornell is widely considered the most transfer-friendly Ivy League school, admitting several hundred more transfer students each year than any of its peers. Historically its transfer acceptance rate has run roughly double its first-year rate. That gap has narrowed recently as applicant volume has climbed: the rate has drifted down from the high-teens a few years ago into the single digits and low double digits in the most recent cycles, even as the raw number of admitted students has stayed in the 650–850 range most years.

Cornell accepts transfer applicants for fall entry only and has paused its spring transfer option in recent cycles, so plan around a single annual intake window.

What Cornell Looks For

  • A clear, specific reason for transferring tied to academic and career goals — vague dissatisfaction with a current school reads poorly
  • Strong performance in college coursework, especially in classes relevant to the intended Cornell major
  • Alignment with the specific college you're applying into — Cornell's undergraduate colleges (Arts & Sciences, Engineering, Human Ecology, ILR, and others) each evaluate transfer fit differently
  • A College Report and Midterm Report confirming good academic and disciplinary standing at your current institution

Requirements & Deadlines

  • Common Application for Transfer, including Cornell's college-specific supplemental essays
  • Priority deadline of March 15 for fall entry
  • One letter of recommendation, ideally from a college instructor
  • College Report and Midterm Report submitted by your current school official
  • Decisions released on a rolling basis from late April through June
