Overview

Purdue University's first-year admissions process is evaluated separately from its transfer process, with its own applicant pool, review committee, and timeline. Most highly selective schools admit first-year applicants at a meaningfully different rate than transfers, so the numbers above should not be assumed to mirror the transfer figures below.

Applicants generally apply through Early Decision, Early Action, or Regular Decision depending on the school's specific offerings, and should weigh the tradeoffs of each round carefully, since binding Early Decision plans and non-binding Early Action or Restrictive Early Action plans have different implications for financial aid and other applications.

What Purdue Looks For

  • A rigorous high school course load, including the most advanced classes available (AP, IB, honors, or dual enrollment)
  • Strong, consistent grades throughout high school, particularly in junior and senior year
  • Meaningful extracurricular involvement, leadership, or personal responsibilities that show depth rather than a long list of shallow activities
  • Thoughtful, authentic essays that reveal genuine voice and self-reflection
  • Strong letters of recommendation from teachers and counselors who know the applicant well

Requirements & Deadlines

  • Common Application, Coalition Application, or the school's own first-year application, submitted by the relevant deadline above
  • Official high school transcript and school profile
  • Letters of recommendation from a counselor and one or more teachers
  • Standardized testing policy varies by school and by year — confirm current SAT/ACT requirements before applying
  • Decisions released according to the applicant's chosen round (Early Decision/Action vs. Regular Decision)

Overview

Purdue's West Lafayette campus has generally maintained a transfer acceptance rate around 40%, admitting a substantial number of transfer students each year across its many colleges and majors.

Purdue accepts transfer applications for both fall and spring terms, with general deadlines of March 1 for fall and October 1 for spring — though individual colleges and majors set their own specific criteria, and admission to some competitive programs can close at any time based on space. Applicants should check Purdue's list of closed programs before applying.

What Purdue Looks For

  • At least 24 semester hours of college-level coursework completed, with a recommended minimum 2.5 GPA (competitive programs expect higher)
  • Coursework aligned with the applicant's intended major — for example, strong math and science grades for Engineering applicants
  • A clear personal statement explaining the applicant's reasons for transferring and why Purdue fits their goals
  • Awareness of program-specific closures, since some competitive majors stop accepting transfer applications once space fills
  • Early submission of transcripts and required materials, since Purdue's process depends on complete files by the deadline

Requirements & Deadlines

  • Purdue's transfer application, due March 1 for fall entry or October 1 for spring entry
  • Official transcripts from every college attended, plus a final high school transcript
  • A personal statement addressing reasons for transferring
  • Program-specific requirements that vary by college and major
  • Decisions released on a rolling basis as applications are completed
