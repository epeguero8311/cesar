Overview

Tufts University's first-year admissions process is evaluated separately from its transfer process, with its own applicant pool, review committee, and timeline. Most highly selective schools admit first-year applicants at a meaningfully different rate than transfers, so the numbers above should not be assumed to mirror the transfer figures below.

Applicants generally apply through Early Decision, Early Action, or Regular Decision depending on the school's specific offerings, and should weigh the tradeoffs of each round carefully, since binding Early Decision plans and non-binding Early Action or Restrictive Early Action plans have different implications for financial aid and other applications.

What Tufts Looks For

  • A rigorous high school course load, including the most advanced classes available (AP, IB, honors, or dual enrollment)
  • Strong, consistent grades throughout high school, particularly in junior and senior year
  • Meaningful extracurricular involvement, leadership, or personal responsibilities that show depth rather than a long list of shallow activities
  • Thoughtful, authentic essays that reveal genuine voice and self-reflection
  • Strong letters of recommendation from teachers and counselors who know the applicant well

Requirements & Deadlines

  • Common Application, Coalition Application, or the school's own first-year application, submitted by the relevant deadline above
  • Official high school transcript and school profile
  • Letters of recommendation from a counselor and one or more teachers
  • Standardized testing policy varies by school and by year — confirm current SAT/ACT requirements before applying
  • Decisions released according to the applicant's chosen round (Early Decision/Action vs. Regular Decision)

Overview

Tufts' transfer acceptance rate has historically swung widely, from as low as 7% to as high as 28% in past cycles, but the most recent data shows the rate tightening considerably — down to around 11% for fall 2025, much closer to Tufts' roughly 10% freshman acceptance rate than in prior years.

Tufts only offers a fall transfer option and does not allow mid-year or spring admission. Prospective transfers apply to the School of Arts and Sciences, the School of Engineering, or the School of the Museum of Fine Arts (BFA programs), and are expected to complete at least two years in residence to earn a Tufts degree.

What Tufts Looks For

  • At least two full-time college semesters completed, with excellent grades — Tufts recommends near-straight-A performance
  • Coursework aligned with the specific school being applied to, whether Arts and Sciences, Engineering, or a BFA program
  • A visual art portfolio (15–20 images or up to 10 minutes of time-based work) for BFA and Combined Degree applicants
  • Engagement beyond the classroom that shows the applicant will contribute to campus life, not just academics
  • A clear, specific rationale for choosing Tufts, addressed directly in the required short-answer essays

Requirements & Deadlines

  • Common Application for Transfer, due mid-March (typically March 15–17) for fall entry
  • Official transcripts from every college attended, plus a final high school transcript
  • Transfer College Report and one academic evaluation from a college-level instructor
  • Visual art portfolio for BFA and Combined Degree applicants, due a few days after the main deadline
  • Decisions released by mid-May; no interviews offered for transfer applicants
